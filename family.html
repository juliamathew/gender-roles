02 / Family, religion & traditions

Where expectations begin.

Family teaches through everyday routines

Children may encounter gender expectations through who prepares meals, manages money, repairs things, or comforts others. Praise and correction can also communicate which behaviors are considered appropriate. These are examples of social learning, not claims that every household follows the same pattern.

Consider an illustrative household where a daughter is asked to clean while a son is encouraged to study. Repeated over time, this division can suggest that care is naturally her responsibility. Rotating chores gives both children chances to learn independence and care.

Religion involves interpretation

Religious life can shape ideas about marriage, dress, caregiving, and leadership. However, a religious tradition is not one uniform voice. Believers may interpret teachings differently, and religious identity interacts with local customs, institutions, and individual choices.

It is important to distinguish a religious text, an institution’s rule, a local practice, and an individual’s belief. Calling all four “religion” hides the differences that help explain why people within the same community may disagree.

Traditions can be meaningful—and debated

Traditions can provide belonging and continuity. They can also raise questions about who is expected to serve, lead, inherit responsibilities, or make decisions. Respectful analysis asks how participants understand a practice and whether people can question it without punishment.

Pew’s India research documents variation in gender attitudes across regions and educational backgrounds. That variation is a reminder to avoid treating a country or a religious community as a single household with identical beliefs.

Change can happen within a family

Families can preserve meaningful celebrations while sharing the work of organizing them. They can encourage children of different genders to learn financial skills, cooking, and emotional expression. These illustrative approaches show that continuity and change do not have to be opposites.

Think about it: Which responsibilities in your household are divided by preference, and which are divided by expectation? Avoid assuming another person’s answer matches yours.
