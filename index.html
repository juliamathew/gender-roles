Gender roles across cultures

A wider view of gender.

Who we are expected to be depends partly on where—and how—we grow up.

Welcome! I’m Julia Mathew, a Rutgers University student. This website explores how gender expectations are learned, reinforced, and questioned through family, religion, traditions, education, work, and media.

My objective is to help visitors compare examples across societies, recognize the difference between a cultural pattern and an individual’s experience, and think critically about expectations that can seem natural simply because they are familiar.

Start with the distinction

Gender roles

Social expectations about behavior, responsibilities, and opportunities associated with gender. A role might involve earning money, providing care, showing emotion, or taking leadership.

Gender stereotypes

Overgeneralized beliefs about what people are like or what they can do because of their gender. A stereotype turns an expectation into a presumed rule.

Culture is not a script

People can accept, negotiate, or reject expectations. Nationality, religion, and gender do not tell us exactly what any individual believes.

How to explore this site

Begin with three case studies, then follow the influences that connect them: home life, institutions, and media. Each topic includes a question for reflection and links to supporting research. The References page explains the evidence and its limits.

Many sources report only women and men. Those categories do not capture every gender identity or family structure. Transgender and nonbinary people also encounter gender expectations, and their experiences should not be treated as interchangeable.
