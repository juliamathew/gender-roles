04 / Media & social media

The stories we see shape possibility.

Repetition can make a role seem normal

Media can influence how audiences understand gender by choosing who appears as an expert, caregiver, leader, or decision-maker. UNESCO identifies media as an important arena for gender equality. Representation includes who gets to speak as well as how people are portrayed.

For an illustrative example, imagine advertisements that always show women cleaning and men explaining financial products. Repetition can make that division feel ordinary even when it does not reflect the range of people’s abilities or lives.

[6] UNESCO ↗

Social media can amplify pressure

UNESCO’s 2024 Gender Report discussion warns that social media can amplify stereotypes and affect girls’ well-being, learning, and career choices. This does not mean every user has the same experience or that every platform interaction is harmful.

A carefully edited feed can make one appearance or lifestyle seem like the standard. Media literacy means asking how content was selected, who benefits from attention, and what experiences are missing.

[5] UNESCO ↗

Visibility can challenge expectations

Media can also present alternatives: fathers providing care, women leading technical teams, and people expressing gender in varied ways. Such examples can make possibilities easier to imagine, although visibility alone does not remove barriers in schools, families, or workplaces.

Cross-cultural sharing can broaden a person’s perspective, but a viral post is not a representative sample of a country. A single creator can describe their own life without speaking for every member of a culture.

Try a four-question media check

  1. Who is visible? Notice who speaks, leads, cares, and makes decisions.
  2. What is assumed? Identify any behavior presented as natural for a gender.
  3. What is missing? Consider whose experiences and perspectives are absent.
  4. What supports the claim? Separate an opinion, an advertisement, and a research finding.
Take it with you: Choose one post or advertisement. Describe what it shows before deciding what it means. Then ask whether your interpretation relies on a stereotype.
